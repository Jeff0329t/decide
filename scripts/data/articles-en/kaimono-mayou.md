How many days has it been sitting in the cart? As the sale's end time approaches you grow impatient, yet your hand stops before the checkout button. And if you don't buy, "I should have bought it after all" stays in your head for a while. Indecision over shopping is small, yet surprisingly draining.

To say it up front: the goal of this page is not "to become a wise consumer." The goal is to shop in a way where, whether you buy or not, you can say "I decided this myself." What's really behind regret is not the item, but how you decided.

## POINT 01 Why you hesitate over a purchase isn't the price

In shopping where the indecision is long, three forces are usually at work.

One is the feeling of wanting it itself. When you picture yourself owning it, or the scene of using it, your heart moves. This is an honest desire, and not a bad thing.

The second is fear of regret. "What if I buy it and don't use it?" "Could I have bought it more cheaply?" Before buying, you're already imagining, in advance, the regret after buying.

The third is the feeling of "I don't want to lose out." Sales, only a few left, limited time. The impatience about missing out is a different thing from the feeling of wanting it, yet when they mix, they become hard to tell apart.

When you're torn, first try separating these three. Depending on whether what's moving you is "wanting," "fear," or "impatience," the action you should take changes.

## POINT 02 Seeing it through the cards

![The tarot card "Four of Pentacles." A figure holding a coin tightly](/assets/rider-waite/pe04-320.webp)

[Four of Pentacles](/cards/pe04.html) is the figure of a person holding a coin tightly and not letting go. Its keywords are "possession," "attachment," and "guarding." In shopping terms, it reflects both sides of the feeling of "I don't want to let go." Is it money you don't want to let go of, or is it the item you don't want to let go of? Depending on which one you want to hold on to, the answer changes.

![The tarot card "Temperance." An angel pouring water between two cups, keeping balance](/assets/rider-waite/ar14-320.webp)

[Temperance](/cards/ar14.html) is the figure of an angel pouring water between two cups. It represents "balance," "moderation," and "adjustment." It's a perspective for finding just the right landing place between the feeling of wanting it and fear. Not buying with all your might, nor enduring and giving up. It makes you check whether there are "options in between," such as renting, an older model, or a lower model.

![The tarot card "Seven of Pentacles." A figure gazing at grown crops](/assets/rider-waite/pe07-320.webp)

[Seven of Pentacles](/cards/pe07.html), in the figure of a person gazing at grown crops, is the perspective of "judging" and "letting time pass." Don't decide right now; set it down overnight, or until the sale ends. If you still want it after time has passed, it's close to a real desire, not impatience. Conversely, if it's something you can forget, then not buying was the right answer.

## POINT 03 Log your "buy" and "don't buy" decisions

Shopping decisions are a theme that goes very well with records. Because the results come out clearly.

Things you bought: did you use them, or not? Things you didn't buy: did you want them later, or forget them? If you leave "buy" or "don't buy" and your reason when you're torn, after a few months your own shopping habits come into view. "My judgment goes soft only during sales." "I tend to make big purchases on nights when I'm tired." Once you know your own patterns, the next decision you're torn over gets shorter.

If you leave it together with your state when you drew a card, you can confirm later that "I was impatient at that time." The more you can leave without a limit on the number of times, from small daily goods to big purchases, the more your history of decisions accumulates. Shopping without regret is not shopping without failure; it's shopping that grows your own way of deciding. Thinking about big purchases in general is also gathered on the page [When you're torn over how to use money](/cards/worry-okane.html).

## POINT 04 Questions to ask yourself

Finally, try asking toward that item now in your cart. Is what's moving you the feeling of wanting it, fear of regret, or impatience about missing out? If there were no sale, would you still want to buy it at the same price? Can you say you still want it after setting it down overnight? If you bought it, can you concretely picture yourself using it? And if you decide not to buy, can you state that reason in one sentence?

It doesn't matter if the answer is "buy" or "don't buy." What matters is the real feeling that you decided in your own words, not from impatience or fear. Shopping with that feeling doesn't drag behind you afterward. Please face what's in your cart once more.
