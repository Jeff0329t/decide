You've read any number of roundup articles about side jobs. You've seen accounts from people who started. Even so, your own first step still isn't decided. "Can I make the time?" "Will it affect my main job?" "What can I even do?" The more you think, the more the anxiety grows, and it becomes "some other time." Does that cycle sound familiar?

To say it up front: the goal of this page is not to push you with "you should start a side job." The goal is to get you to a state where, whether you start or hold off, you can say you decided for yourself, having checked what needs checking. Once you know what's really behind the anxiety, the decision you're torn over becomes much smaller.

In this article, we sort out five things to check before you start. The cards are used not to tell whether things will go well, but as a tool for looking at your own thinking again from a different angle.

## Being torn over a side job is a sign you're thinking carefully

The reasons people are torn over a side job are mostly things like the following.

- Your main job is busy, and you're not confident you can keep it up
- You don't know where to start, or what you can do
- You're concerned about the procedural side, like company rules and taxes

All of these are worries that come up precisely because you're thinking seriously. Rather than starting on momentum alone, checking these worries one by one before you start makes it easier to continue for longer in the end.

## Thing to check 1: Why do you want a side job?

The purpose of a side job differs from person to person.

1. You want to increase your income
2. You want to build skills and experience you can't get from your main job
3. You want to prepare to eventually go independent
4. You want to turn something you like into work, even on a small scale

Depending on your purpose, the kind of side job you should choose, and how much time you should spend on it, will change. The feeling of "somehow I'm just anxious" is also an important starting point, but if you put it into words one step further, it becomes a basis for judgment when you're torn.

## Thing to check 2: Can you secure time to keep going steadily?

![The tarot card "Eight of Pentacles." A craftsperson carefully finishing each piece, one by one](/assets/rider-waite/pe08-320.webp)

[Eight of Pentacles](/cards/pe08.html) is drawn as a craftsperson carefully finishing each piece, one by one. Its keywords are "training," "focus," and "steady effort."

With a side job, results rarely come right after you start. From the perspective of Eight of Pentacles, try asking yourself this.

> How much time each week could I keep using for this side job? Can I picture myself continuing that for half a year?

Write out concretely how many hours a week you could continue without strain, and whether that's weekday nights or weekends. A plan that cuts too deeply into sleep or time with family is hard to sustain.

## Thing to check 3: Is there any problem with your main job or company rules?

Before starting a side job, check the procedural side as well.

- Do your workplace's work rules allow side jobs?
- Would the content compete with your main job?
- Do you understand how taxes will be handled if income comes in, such as filing a tax return?

Because the detailed conditions differ by company and situation, check your work rules, and consult a specialist if needed. If you start while leaving this vague, it becomes a seed of major anxiety later.

## Thing to check 4: Can you try small, and judge partway through?

![The tarot card "Seven of Pentacles." A figure gazing at crops that are growing](/assets/rider-waite/pe07-320.webp)

[Seven of Pentacles](/cards/pe07.html) is a card showing a figure who has stopped their hands before crops that are growing, and gazes at them. Its keywords are "judging," "waiting for growth," and "mid-course evaluation."

A side job doesn't need to start big from the beginning.

1. First, try it small, for just one month
2. After three months, review whether to continue, change how you do it, or stop
3. At the review, include not only income but also "enjoyment" and "learning" in the evaluation

Deciding from the start that "it's okay to quit" lowers the hurdle to starting considerably.

## Thing to check 5: Can you picture what lies beyond?

![The tarot card "Two of Wands." A figure holding a globe, looking out into the distance](/assets/rider-waite/wa02-320.webp)

[Two of Wands](/cards/wa02.html) is the figure of a person holding a globe and looking out into the distance, and its keywords are "planning," "outlook," and "decision."

Try picturing the self you'd be glad to be, beyond continuing the side job. A self with one more pillar of income, a self relied on in your main job too for a new skill, a self who holds independence as an option. When you can picture the outlook, it's easier to feel meaning in the steady work in front of you.

## Judge the results of trying, by your records

In the try-it-small approach, the most important thing is not to judge by "feel" on the review day. The response right after you start is heavily swayed by your mood that day.

So leave a record of the period while you're trying it. The time you used, the income that came in, whether it was enjoyable or tough. If you open that record on the review day, your judgment of "continue, change, or stop" can be made based on facts, not momentum or regret. As your record of decisions builds up, your own "form you can continue in" comes into view. A side-job decision is not a one-time thing; it's an accumulation of small reviews. What supports that accumulation is the record.

## Finally

There is no answer about whether to start a side job that is right for everyone. What matters is to check your purpose, time, rules, way of trying, and outlook, one by one, and decide in a way you can accept as your own. And once you've started, to keep reviewing while looking at your records. The step that graduates you from "some other time" begins with that checking work.
