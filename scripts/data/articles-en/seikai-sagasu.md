You read reviews of the company you might move to, you go back and forth between comparison sites, and every time you try to decide, a new source of anxiety turns up. "I don't want to fail." "Maybe there is a better option." You keep searching for the right answer for days like that, and yet you don't feel any closer to it. Have you fallen into that state?

Let me say this first: the goal of this article is not to learn a better way to search for the right answer. The goal is to let go of the very assumption that "the right answer is out there somewhere." When that assumption comes off, deciding becomes surprisingly light.

## POINT 01 Why the more you research, the less you can decide

Over long years at school, we were trained on tests to search for "one correct answer." If we carry that habit into adulthood, we come to assume that for life's choices, too, an objective right answer must be prepared somewhere.

But for life's choices, that right answer does not exist. If you change jobs, there may be an unexpected reorganization; if you stay, you may find an unexpected sense of purpose. With an infinite number of future variables, it is not possible to work out "the absolutely correct path" in advance by logic alone.

And if you still keep researching, you see the strengths and weaknesses of each option too clearly, and you become even less able to move. This state, where gathering information leaves you more torn, is also called "analysis paralysis." The problem is not the amount of information. It is the assumption that "there is a right answer I have to hit."

Try reversing the idea. The right answer does not exist "before you choose." It is something you grow after you choose. If it is a path you chose with your own sense of acceptance, then even if you stumble along the way, you can work through it — "because I decided this myself." Conversely, a path chosen only by gain and loss turns into regret — "I should have gone with the other one" — at the slightest setback. I also sorted out the roots of the state of being unable to decide in [Why you can't decide when you're indecisive, and how to find your way out](/articles/kimerarenai).

One thing I don't want you to misunderstand: "stopping the search for the right answer" does not mean "deciding without thinking." Gathering information itself matters. The problem is continuing to search even after you have enough material, thinking "there must still be something I've missed." Gathering the information you need and gathering information to put off your anxiety look similar, but they are completely different. The guide is simple: when you feel "no matter how much more I research from here, the material for my judgment will not increase," that is the point to stop researching. After that, it is time to look not at material that will not increase, but at your own feelings.

## POINT 02 What the cards help you see

![Tarot card "The Hanged Man." A figure hanging upside down, quietly waiting](/assets/rider-waite/ar12-320.webp)

[The Hanged Man](/cards/ar12.html) shows a figure hanging upside down by one foot. At first glance it looks like a punishment, but the expression is calm. What this card stands for is "the view you can only see by stopping." When you stop the hands that are anxiously searching around for the right answer, just once, the shape of the problem looks different from an upside-down perspective. Time when you cannot move is not wasted. It is time for your way of seeing to change.

![Tarot card "The Lovers." Two figures, and an angel watching over them](/assets/rider-waite/ar06-320.webp)

[The Lovers](/cards/ar06.html) is a card that depicts "choice" itself. What is asked here is not "which is correct," but "which fits my own values?" The less a choice can be settled by the merits of its conditions, the more the last ruler that remains is how well it fits you. When you are tired of searching for the right answer, this card encourages you to change the ruler you are holding.

![Tarot card "Two of Swords." A blindfolded woman crossing two swords](/assets/rider-waite/sw02-320.webp)

[Two of Swords](/cards/sw02.html) shows a blindfolded woman crossing two swords. It stands for a state of holding a decision back and trying to keep balance. A head that has gathered too much information is actually like this blindfold: under the reason "I still don't know," it may be averting its eyes from what it should be looking at. It is a card that asks what you are currently trying not to see.

## POINT 03 Decide the direction with intuition, and grow it with a record

For a choice you can accept, it matters to separate the roles of "intuition" and "logic." The role of intuition is to decide the direction — the work of grasping your true feeling of "what do I really want to do?" The role of logic is to decide how to proceed — designing how to carry out the direction you chose while reducing risk. The pattern that tends to fail is trying to calculate even the direction by logic. Comparing specs alone will never bring your true feelings out.

Even so, your true feelings are hard to find by searching inside your head alone. A card becomes a "mirror for thinking" for that purpose. The subtle reaction at the moment you see the card you drew — "relieved," "a little put off" — is itself a clue to the true feeling that was hidden under reason. What matters is that the card does not produce the answer. It is a tool for observing your own reaction.

And once you decide, leave just one line about your reason in a log. If "why I chose this path" remains, then when what you're torn over returns along the way, you can look back and check. A record is the foundation for the work of growing the path you chose into the right answer. I also wrote about a setup that keeps you from blaming yourself later in [How to choose without regret](/articles/koukai-shinai-erabikata). The logs that build up little by little with each decision eventually turn into confidence — "I am someone who chooses by standards like these."

## POINT 04 Questions to ask yourself

Finally, try asking yourself these questions. Who do you think prepared the "right answer" you are searching for now? If you stopped your researching hands, what true feeling would surface first? With gain and loss all set aside, which path is your heart facing? Even if difficulties appear, which one could you state clearly was "decided by me"?

From a life of choosing the right answer, to a life of making what you chose into the right answer. Today's decision can be the first step of that. Leave one line about your reason, and start walking the path you chose.
