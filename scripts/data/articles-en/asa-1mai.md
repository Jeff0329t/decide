In the morning, from the moment you stop your alarm, your head is already noisy. Emails you haven't replied to, work you want to finish by the end of today, a small errand you couldn't settle last night. "Which job should I clear first today?" "How should I write that reply?" You have things you want to do, but without knowing what to start with, before you notice, the morning is over. Have you had a morning like that?

To say it up front: the goal of this article is not to perfect your morning task management. The goal is to decide just one thing, in the single minute before work or in a gap in the housework: "what theme will I spend today, this one day, with?" A morning where the theme is decided begins surprisingly quietly.

## POINT 01 Decide how you'll be, not just what you'll do

A to-do list is a list of "tasks you should do." But what's really behind morning indecision is not the amount of tasks itself. It's "is today a day to push forward, or a day to tidy things up?" "Is it a day to face people, or a day to immerse myself in one task?" Because your stance for the day isn't decided, you end up hesitating over each and every small choice.

A one-card tarot draw in the morning is a switch for deciding this stance. It is not for predicting how your day will go. You ask, "What perspective do I need today?" draw one card, and convert the keyword of the card that comes up into one line as your guideline for today. That's all there is to it.

The way to do it is simple. First, ask the question in your mind. Next, draw one card by intuition. Finally, tie the keyword that came up to today's actions. For example, if a card that values balance comes up, "today is a day not to cram things in, but to wrap up at about eighty percent." If a card pointing to the power to move forward comes up, "today is the day to push that proposal I've been torn over all at once." The trick is to translate abstract words into your own words for today.

There is also meaning in it being the morning. At night you drag the day's events with you, and it's a time when feelings tend to swell. It's not suited to deciding a theme. Precisely because it's the morning, when your head is still quiet and before today's schedule starts moving, you can set the standard for the day smoothly.

Once one theme is decided, a "ruler" is born for your judgments during the day. Even if a sudden request comes in, you can judge on the spot: "today is a day for tidying up, so if it's not urgent, I'll politely move it to another day." The reason the time you spend stopping at each decision decreases is that you placed a standard at the start of the day. The feeling of getting tired from the sheer number of choices in a day is also sorted out in [Why decision fatigue leaves you unable to decide, and how to recover](/articles/kettei-dekinai-tsukareta).

## POINT 02 Seeing it through the cards

![The tarot card "The Sun." A child on a white horse under the sun](/assets/rider-waite/ar19-320.webp)

[The Sun](/cards/ar19.html) is the figure of a child on a white horse under a large sun. It represents "clarity," "affirmation," and "honesty." On a morning when this card comes up, it's a day you can get through without overthinking. Don't brace yourself too hard; move forward on what's in front of you with a bright feeling. That simplicity becomes today's theme.

![The tarot card "Ace of Wands." A single sprouting wand offered from the clouds](/assets/rider-waite/wa01-320.webp)

[Ace of Wands](/cards/wa01.html) is the figure of a sprouting wand offered from within the clouds. It represents "the power to begin," "the first step," and "a bud of passion." If there's something you've been thinking about for a long time but haven't been able to start, this is a signal to make it the first item of today. You don't decide a big plan; you decide only the first step.

![The tarot card "The Magician." A figure pointing to the sky, with tools laid out on a table](/assets/rider-waite/ar01-320.webp)

[The Magician](/cards/ar01.html) is a figure pointing to heaven and earth, with every tool laid out on the table. It represents "starting with what you have on hand" and "the power to decide for yourself." Rather than counting what you're missing, what can you do with what you already have? On a morning when this card comes up, it's a day not to use being underprepared as an excuse.

## POINT 03 Don't just draw and stop. Logging turns your morning minute into an asset

However, if you just draw one card and think "I see," the theme will have disappeared before noon. What really makes a morning one-card draw work is the small extra step of logging. You leave just one line in your decision log with the card you drew and the theme for today that you decided from it. Something like "The Sun. Today I'll move forward honestly, without taking too much on."

Once a log remains, you can look back at night. Days you spent in line with the theme, and days you were swept away. As you line up themes and results and look back over them, your own patterns come into view. "On Mondays things run better if I keep the theme light." "On days when I meet people, if I set a focused-work theme, it falls apart." These realizations can't be gained from a habit of drawing and being done.

Moreover, the accumulation of logs also works on the mornings from the next day onward. Because you can see what theme yesterday's self set and how you spent the day, the precision of the question you ask when you draw today's card goes up. The morning minute becomes not a point, but a line. Draw, decide, leave a record, look back. The reason to use an app as the place to turn this small cycle is here.

The trick to continuing is to lower the hurdle. Draw just one card, log just one line. Because it's a length that fits in the time it takes to brew coffee, it doesn't break even on busy mornings. You don't need a careful review. Even if just one line remains, it's proof that you faced your theme that morning.

## POINT 04 Questions to ask yourself

Tomorrow morning, when you wake up, try asking yourself this. What do you want to value most today: moving forward, tidying up, or facing people? If you had to say that answer in one phrase, what would it be? Having decided your theme, how do you imagine you'll feel when you end the day at night?

Big decisions, too, are ultimately an accumulation of small daily choices. Change a morning that begins by being swept along into a morning that begins with your own theme. Try placing that quiet one minute in tomorrow morning.
