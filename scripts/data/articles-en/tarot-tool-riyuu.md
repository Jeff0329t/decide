Should I change jobs, or stay at my current company? Should I start a new challenge, or build up my strength for now? And before all that, which task should I start with today? Every day, we stand before choices large and small. We pile up research trying to find the right answer, we are swayed by other people's opinions, and in the end we greet the night having decided nothing. I myself was troubled for a long time by that "decision fatigue."

To begin with, every day we make an enormous number of decisions, large and small combined. In an age overflowing with information, the more you try to find the right answer, the more time research takes from you; the more you compare, the more options increase; other people's success stories catch your eye, and you lose the room to think under your own conditions. The feeling of "I want someone to decide for me" is born as the other side of that tiredness. But with an answer decided by someone else, you cannot feel at peace when the result is bad. That is why what I needed was not someone to give me the answer, but something to reflect my true feelings back to me.

"I want someone to push my back. But I can't feel at peace with an answer someone else decided." At the end of that conflict, I arrived at a way of using tarot not as "a tool for telling," but as "a tool for thinking." This article is a record of the starting point of that way of thinking.

## POINT 01 Tarot was not a "prediction," but a "mirror"

To be honest, I was not originally the type to believe in fortune-telling that claims to foretell the future. Even if I turned a card and was told "your future will be like this," it somehow felt like someone else's business.

The turning point was paying attention to the pictures of tarot themselves. In the 78 cards, patterns of the feelings, trials, and conflicts that people experience in life — beginnings and endings, being torn and deciding, losing and gaining — are gathered as pictures. It is, so to speak, a "picture book of states of mind."

When you stand before this picture book, something curious happens. When you draw a card while you're torn about something, "what was I really afraid of?" surfaces. When you draw a card for each of options A and B, you can sort out "which choice's feelings do I want to face?" The cards did not tell the future. The cards' pictures and keywords become the trigger that draws out your true feelings, which had not yet become words. A mirror shows a different expression depending on your own state. Even with the same card, the words you receive change with how you feel that day. That is not vagueness. It is proof that your inner self has moved. I also sorted out the mechanism of being unable to decide itself in [Why you can't decide when you're indecisive, and how to find your way out](/articles/kimerarenai).

## POINT 02 What the cards help you see

![Tarot card "The Magician." A figure pointing to the sky, with tools laid out on a table](/assets/rider-waite/ar01-320.webp)

[The Magician](/cards/ar01.html) shows a figure with every tool laid out on a table, pointing to the sky and the earth. It stands for the stance of "the materials are all here. All that remains is to assemble them with your own hands." The core of tarot as a decision-making tool is here. The answer is not given from outside. It is in your own hands. It is a card that reminds you of that fact.

![Tarot card "The Hermit." A sage standing alone on a snowy mountain, holding a lantern](/assets/rider-waite/ar09-320.webp)

[The Hermit](/cards/ar09.html) shows a sage standing alone, holding a lantern. It stands for "looking inward" and "the quiet of searching for your own answer." Time to step away from the noise of other people's opinions and reputations, and listen to your own inner voice. It is a symbol of the act of thinking for yourself, rather than having someone decide for you.

![Tarot card "The Fool." A young person walking lightly at the edge of a cliff with a single bundle](/assets/rider-waite/ar00-320.webp)

[The Fool](/cards/ar00.html) shows a young person taking a light step forward with a single bundle. It stands for "a step with no right answer" and "the freedom of a beginning." From a state where you cannot move until perfect information is in place, you take a first step. That the first card of tarot shows this figure expresses the essence of decision-making well.

## POINT 03 A perspective alone is not enough. Why you "record" the decision

However, even if you gain a perspective from the cards, that alone does not move a decision forward. A perspective is only a trigger; decision-making goes as far as deciding, acting, and facing the result. That is where a record of the decision becomes necessary.

You keep the card you drew, what you were torn about at the time, and the decision you made yourself, in a decision log. Then, after time has passed, you can look back accurately at "why did I decide that at the time?" What comes into view is not whether the result was good, but the habits of how you decide. "When my anxiety is strong, I tend to put decisions off." "Things I decided on a morning when I felt well leave little regret." Insights like these cannot be gained without a record.

As the records of looking back build up, another change occurs. The question you felt at every decision — "was this really all right?" — changes into a reference: "I was torn in a similar way before. At that time, I decided like this, and it turned out like this." When the true nature of anxiety changes from "an unprecedented big event" to "a pattern I have already experienced," the temperature of what you're torn about goes down.

When I made DECIDE., there was a belief I absolutely did not want to waver on. "The cards do not decide the answer. You are the one who decides." The app never instructs you to "choose A." All the cards show is questions and perspectives. That is precisely why what remains in the record is not "the result of a reading," but "your own decision," and that accumulation becomes material for your next decision. Draw, decide, keep, look back. DECIDE. exists as a tool for turning that cycle.

## POINT 04 Questions to ask yourself

Finally, let me ask you, who are torn right now. What is it that you cannot quite decide at the moment? Deep inside what you're torn about, are your feelings not already leaning to one side? And if you are afraid of failing, is what you fear the result itself, or is it accepting that "I decided this myself"?

Being torn arises as proof that you are thinking seriously about your own life. Instead of having someone decide the answer for you, try drawing one card as a mirror to reflect your true feelings. The one who decides is always you.
